# ADR 0003: LoRA on bf16 weights, quantize afterwards

**Status:** accepted, 2026-10-30

## Context

There are three options on one 32 GB Mac:

1. full fine-tuning;
2. LoRA on the bf16 base;
3. QLoRA, which is LoRA on a 4-bit base.

The deliverable is a model that runs fast locally, so the 4-bit version is the one people would actually use.

## Decision

LoRA (rank 16, every layer, `mlx_lm` scale 2.0, prompt tokens masked from the loss) on the bf16 base. Then fuse the adapter and convert to 8-bit and 4-bit with `mlx_lm.convert`.

## Why

- **Full fine-tune:** a 3B model in bf16 with Adam state needs roughly 3B × (2 + 2 + 8) bytes ≈ 36 GB before activations, which doesn't fit. It would also produce a 6 GB artifact per experiment instead of a ~40 MB adapter.
- **QLoRA:** cheaper to train, but it fixes the quantization before training, so "how much does quantization cost?" can't be measured cleanly. Training in bf16 and quantizing afterwards lets bf16, 8-bit and 4-bit of the *same* fine-tune be evaluated side by side.
- **LoRA on bf16:** peaks at about 10 GB and takes about 90 minutes per epoch on 6.4k examples. That is cheap enough to also run the learning-curve and rank ablations.

## Consequences

- The quantization chart isolates post-training quantization. It does not show whether QLoRA would have recovered some of the loss, which is left as an open question in the README.
- The rank ablation keeps `scale` fixed at 2.0 (mlx_lm multiplies the LoRA output by `scale` directly, with no `alpha / r`). Higher ranks therefore also mean a larger effective update. That is the standard mlx_lm behaviour and is noted next to the chart.
